Two levels of parliament, two different members

Your federal member

Elected to the House of Representatives in Canberra to represent one local electorate. Federal members take part in debate on proposed national laws, and the party or coalition with the support of most members forms the government. Members face election at least every three years.

Your state member

Elected to your state parliament to represent a district, which covers a different area from your federal electorate. State parliaments make laws for their own state and run on their own election timetables. Your state member is a separate person from your federal member.

Based on information from the Parliament of Australia and the Parliamentary Education Office.

About this site

Why might I see more than one federal member?

Postcodes weren’t designed around electoral boundaries, so one postcode can cover parts of several electorates. We show every member whose electorate overlaps your postcode. To confirm which electorate you live in, use the Australian Electoral Commission’s Find my electorate tool.

Where does the information come from?

Names, parties, electorates, roles and photos come from OpenAustralia, a project that publishes parliamentary information. It can lag behind recent changes such as by-elections or ministry reshuffles.

Who runs this site?

It’s an independent project. It isn’t run by, or connected to, any government body, political party or candidate, and it doesn’t save or share the postcodes you enter.
